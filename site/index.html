A rising employee-cost share cost ₹178 crore

Gross margin and other expenses both improved as a share of revenue. Employee cost grew 33.5% against revenue growth of 15.9%, which cancelled those gains.

Hypothesis: new stores were staffed before they reached full sales

58 of 85 new stores opened in Q4. Revenue per square foot of year-end space fell 3.2% (₹33,599 → ₹32,509), and employee cost per store rose from ₹2.44 crore to ₹2.70 crore. Testing this needs store-level data by opening date, which only DMart has.

Three levers, about ₹258 crore in the base case

1

Staff to the ramp

Win back 10 of the 27 bps employee-cost rise by staffing new stores to a sales ramp curve and sharing staff across nearby stores.

+₹67 cr

2

Shift the mix

Add 10 bps of gross margin by growing general merchandise and apparel, without raising everyday prices.

+₹67 cr

3

Ramp new stores faster

Close half the sales-density gap: ₹1,123 cr extra revenue at 11.1% flow-through.

+₹124 cr

Risks

  • Mix changes must not weaken DMart's low-price image
  • Leaner staffing can hurt service, shrinkage and availability
  • New-store ramp depends on location
  • Assumptions are untested against DMart's internal data

Next steps

  • Get store-level sales and staff hours by opening month
  • Build a staffing-to-sales curve for new stores
  • Check gross margin by category and region
  • Pilot the staffing model in one city cluster
